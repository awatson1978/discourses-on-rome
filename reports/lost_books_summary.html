Book 49

Plutarch: Cato Major 26-27 · Dio: frr. book 21 (Zonaras 9.26-27)
  • The Third Punic War began in the 602nd year from the founding of the city and was completed within the fifth year of its start. war
  • M. Porcius Cato and Scipio Nasica contended with opposing opinions, Cato urging war and that Carthage be removed and destroyed, Nasica arguing against it. domestic-politics
  • It was resolved to declare war on the Carthaginians because they had ships contrary to the treaty, had led an army outside their borders, had borne arms against Rome's ally and friend Masinissa, and had refused to admit his son Gulussa into their city. war
  • Before any forces were embarked, envoys from Utica came to Rome surrendering themselves and all they had, an omen welcome to the senators and bitter to the Carthaginians. foreign-diplomacy
  • Games were held to Dis Pater at Tarentum in accordance with the Sibylline books, which had last been held a hundred years before, in the First Punic War, in the 502nd year of the city. prodigy-religion
  • Thirty envoys came to Rome through whom the Carthaginians surrendered themselves, but Cato's opinion prevailed that the decree stand and the consuls set out for war as soon as possible. foreign-diplomacy
  • The consuls, after receiving 300 hostages and all arms and instruments of war at Carthage, ordered on the senate's authority that the Carthaginians build their town in another place no less than ten miles from the sea, driving them by this indignity to war. war
  • Carthage began to be besieged and assaulted by the consuls L. Marcius and M'. Manilius. war
  • In the assault Scipio extricated two tribunes who had rashly broken in with their cohorts, freed a Roman fort attacked by night with the help of a few horsemen, and won the chief glory for relieving the besieged camp. war
  • When the consul's army was routed by Hasdrubal in an unfavorable pass, as Scipio had warned, Scipio returned with a few squadrons of cavalry, freed the two besieged cohorts, and brought them back unharmed. war
  • Cato declared in the senate that the others serving in Africa flitted about as shadows while Scipio was full of vigor, and the Roman people so embraced him that most tribes voted him consul though his age did not permit it. domestic-politics
  • When the tribune L. Scribonius proposed that the Lusitanians sold into slavery in Gaul by Ser. Galba be restored to freedom, Cato urged it fiercely, but Galba, pleading pitiably while embracing his young sons and his ward, secured the rejection of the bill. trial
  • Andriscus, a man of lowest station calling himself Philippus and son of king Perseus, escaped secretly from Rome, gathered an army with an invented tale of his royal birth, and occupied all Macedonia by consent or by arms. war
