*Era: 292–219 BCE — Third Samnite aftermath, Pyrrhic War, First Punic War, interwar years.*

The consul Cn. Cornelius, surrounded by the Punic fleet and lured out as if to a parley, was captured by treachery [17.1]. The consul C. Duillius fought with success against the Carthaginian fleet, and first of all Roman commanders celebrated a triumph for a victory at sea; for which there was granted him the perpetual honour that, when he returned from dinner, a torch should be carried and a flute-player should play before him [17.2]. The consul L. Cornelius fought with success in Sardinia and Corsica against the Sardinians, the Corsicans, and Hanno, commander of the Carthaginians [17.3]. Hannibal, the Carthaginian commander, when the fleet he commanded had been defeated, was crucified by his own soldiers [17.5]. The consul Atilius Regulus, after defeating the Carthaginians in a battle at sea, crossed over into Africa [17.6].
