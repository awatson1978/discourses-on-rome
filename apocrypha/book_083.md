Flavius Fimbria in Asia, after routing several of Mithridates's prefects in battle, took the city of Pergamum, and, having laid siege to the king, came not far from capturing him [83.1]. He stormed and destroyed the city of Ilium, which was reserving itself for Sulla's authority, and recovered a great part of Asia [83.2]. Sulla cut down the Thracians in several battles [83.3].

While Lucius Cinna and Gnaeus Papirius Carbo, who had made themselves consuls for two years, were preparing war against Sulla, it was brought about, through Lucius Valerius Flaccus, princeps of the senate, and those who were eager for concord, that envoys should be sent to Sulla concerning peace [83.4]. Cinna was killed by his own army, which he was forcing, against its will, to board ship and march against Sulla [83.5]. Carbo held the consulship alone [83.6].

Sulla, having crossed into Asia, made peace with Mithridates, on condition that the king withdraw from the provinces of Asia, Bithynia, and Cappadocia [83.7]. Fimbria, deserted by his army, which had gone over to Sulla, stabbed himself, and, offering his neck, prevailed upon his own slave to kill him [83.8].
