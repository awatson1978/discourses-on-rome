Adherbal, attacked by Jugurtha and besieged in the town of Cirta, was killed by him against the warning of the senate; and for this cause war was declared upon Jugurtha [64.1]. The consul Calpurnius Bestia, ordered to wage it, made peace with Jugurtha without the order of the people and the senate [64.2].

Jugurtha, summoned under public pledge to name those whose counsels he had followed — for he was said to have bribed many in the senate — came to Rome [64.3]. Facing a capital charge over the murder of the princeling Massiva, he fled the city secretly, and is reported to have said as he went that Rome was a city for sale, and would soon perish if it found a buyer [64.4].

The legate Aulus Postumius, after an unsuccessful battle against Jugurtha, added to it a shameful peace, which the senate ruled was not to be kept [64.5].
