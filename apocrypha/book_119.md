In Asia, Gaius Trebonius was killed by the treachery of Publius Dolabella [119.1]. For that crime Dolabella was judged a public enemy by the senate [119.2].

Before Mutina, when the consul Pansa had fought without success against Antonius, the consul Aulus Hirtius came up with his army, routed the forces of Marcus Antonius, and made the fortune of the two sides equal [119.3]. Antonius, defeated by Hirtius and by Caesar, fled into Gaul, joined to himself Marcus Lepidus with the legions under his command, and was declared an enemy by the senate, together with all who were within his lines [119.4]. Aulus Hirtius, who after the victory fell in the enemy's camp, and Gaius Pansa, who died of the wound received in the unsuccessful battle, were buried in the Campus Martius [119.5].

The senate showed itself ungrateful toward Gaius Caesar, the sole survivor of the three leaders: for to Decimus Brutus, freed from the siege of Mutina by Caesar, it decreed the honor of a triumph, while of Caesar and his soldiers it made insufficient acknowledgment [119.6]. Whereupon Gaius Caesar, reconciled with Marcus Antonius through Marcus Lepidus, came to Rome with his army, and, his opponents cowed by his coming, was elected consul in the nineteenth year of his age [119.7].
