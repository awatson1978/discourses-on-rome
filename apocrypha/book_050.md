Thessaly, when Pseudophilippus would have invaded and occupied it, was defended through Roman envoys and the auxiliaries of the Achaeans [50.1]. Prusias, king of Bithynia, a man of the basest vices, was killed by his son Nicomedes, with the aid of Attalus, king of Pergamum [50.2]. Of the three Roman envoys sent to make peace between Nicomedes and Prusias—one scarred upon the head, one lame of foot, one dull of wit—M. Cato said that the embassy had neither head nor feet nor heart [50.3]. In Syria, while its slothful king lay about taverns and brothels, Hammonius ruled; and through him all the king's friends were killed, and queen Laodice, and Antigonus the son of Demetrius [50.4]. Masinissa, king of Numidia, died, having lived more than ninety years, a distinguished man; so vigorous was he even in old age that after his eighty-sixth year he begot a son [50.5]. Because Masinissa had left his kingdom jointly to his three sons, Micipsa, Gulussa, and Mastanabal, and had bidden P. Scipio Aemilianus divide it, Scipio apportioned the administration of the kingdom among them [50.6]. Scipio persuaded Phameas Himilco, prefect of the Carthaginian cavalry, a brave man whose service the Carthaginians particularly relied upon, to come over to the Romans with his horse [50.7]. Of the three envoys sent to Masinissa, M. Claudius Marcellus was overwhelmed by the waves in a storm that arose [50.8]. The Carthaginians killed in their senate-house Hasdrubal, grandson of Masinissa, whom they had as praetor, suspicion of treason falling upon him because he was kinsman to Gulussa, who was aiding the Romans [50.9]. P. Scipio Aemilianus, while a candidate for the aedileship, was declared consul by the people; and since his years did not lawfully allow it, he was exempted from the laws, after a great struggle between the plebs who strove for him and the fathers who resisted [50.10]. M'. Manilius stormed several cities set about Carthage [50.11]. Pseudophilippus in Macedonia, after cutting down the praetor P. Juventius with his army, was defeated and captured by Q. Caecilius, and Macedonia was recovered [50.12].
