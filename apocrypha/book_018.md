*Era: 292–219 BCE — Third Samnite aftermath, Pyrrhic War, First Punic War, interwar years.*

Atilius Regulus in Africa killed a serpent of portentous size, with great loss of his own soldiers [18.1]. Though he had fought several battles against the Carthaginians with success, Regulus complained by letter to the senate that no successor was sent him; and among his reasons he alleged that his little farm had been abandoned by its hired hands [18.2]. He was defeated in battle and captured by Xanthippus, a Lacedaemonian commander whom the Carthaginians had summoned [18.3]. Elsewhere operations were conducted with success by all the Roman commanders, on land and on sea; but the shipwrecks of the fleets marred them [18.4]. Tib. Coruncanius was the first man of the plebs created pontifex maximus [18.5]. The censors M'. Valerius Maximus and P. Sempronius Sophus, in revising the roll of the senate, removed sixteen men from that body [18.6]. They closed the lustrum, in which two hundred and ninety-seven thousand seven hundred and ninety-seven heads of citizens were counted [18.7]. Regulus, sent by the Carthaginians to the senate to treat for peace, or failing that for an exchange of captives, and bound by oath to return, advised the senate against both; then, keeping his faith, he returned, and perished under the tortures of the Carthaginians [18.8].
