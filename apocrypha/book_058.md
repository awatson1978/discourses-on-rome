*Era: 167–91 BCE — post-Pydna Mediterranean hegemony, Carthage and Corinth destroyed, the Gracchi, Jugurtha, Cimbri, the road to the Social War.*

The tribune of the plebs Tib. Sempronius Gracchus, against the will of the senate and of the equestrian order, carried an agrarian law: that no one should hold more than a thousand iugera of the public land [58.1]. Carried on by his frenzy, he deposed by law his colleague M. Octavius, who defended the opposing cause; and he had himself, his brother C. Gracchus, and his father-in-law Appius Claudius created triumvirs for dividing the land [58.2]. He promulgated a second agrarian law, by which to open more land for himself: that the same triumvirs should judge what land was public and what private [58.3]. Then, when there was less land than could be divided without offending the plebs, he announced that he would propose a law, that the money of king Attalus be distributed among those who were entitled to land under the Sempronian law [58.4]. For Attalus, king of Pergamum, the son of Eumenes, had left the Roman people his heir [58.5]. The ex-consul T. Annius, having spoken against Gracchus in the senate, was dragged by him before the people; and from the rostra he harangued against Gracchus once more [58.6]. When Gracchus sought to be made tribune a second time, he was killed on the Capitol by the optimates, at the instigation of P. Cornelius Nasica, struck first with the fragments of a bench; and he was thrown unburied into the river, among the others killed in the sedition [58.7]. The book also contained operations, with varying outcome, against the runaway slaves in Sicily [58.8].
