The tribune Publius Sulpicius, at the instigation of Gaius Marius, promulgated pernicious laws: that the exiles be recalled, that the new citizens and the freedmen be distributed into the thirty-five tribes, and that Gaius Marius be made commander against Mithridates, king of Pontus [77.1]. When Sulpicius had used violence against the consuls who opposed him, and Quintus Pompeius, son of the consul Quintus Pompeius and son-in-law of Sulla, had been killed, the consul Lucius Sulla came into the city with an army, fought in the city itself against the faction of Sulpicius and Marius, and drove it out [77.2]. Twelve men of that faction, among them Gaius Marius, father and son, were adjudged public enemies by the senate [77.3].

Publius Sulpicius, hiding in a villa, was dragged out upon the information of his own slave, and killed; the slave, freed that he might have the promised reward, was then thrown from the rock for the crime of betraying his master [77.4]. Gaius Marius the son crossed over to Africa [77.5]. Gaius Marius the father hid in the marshes of Minturnae and was dragged out by the townsmen; and when a Gallic slave, sent to kill him, drew back in terror before the majesty of so great a man, Marius was placed upon a public ship and carried to Africa [77.6].

Lucius Sulla set in order the constitution of the state, and then led out colonies [77.7]. The consul Quintus Pompeius, having set out to receive the army from the proconsul Gnaeus Pompeius, was killed by the latter's design [77.8].

Mithridates, king of Pontus, having seized Bithynia and Cappadocia and driven out the legate Aquilius, entered Phrygia, a province of the Roman people, with a huge army [77.9].
