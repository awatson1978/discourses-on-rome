Lucius Apuleius Saturninus, aided by Gaius Marius, and after his rival Aulus Nunnius had been killed by soldiers, was made tribune of the plebs by violence, and he bore the tribunate as violently as he had sought it [69.1]. After he had carried an agrarian law by force and prosecuted Metellus Numidicus for not swearing to it, Metellus went into voluntary exile at Rhodes, where he occupied himself in hearing and reading great men [69.2]. Upon his departure Gaius Marius, the author of the sedition, who had bought his sixth consulship with money scattered through the tribes, interdicted him from water and fire [69.3].

The same tribune Apuleius Saturninus killed Gaius Memmius, a candidate for the consulship, because he feared him as an opponent of his measures [69.4]. The senate being roused by these deeds, and Marius having gone over to its cause, Saturninus was crushed by arms and killed in a kind of war, together with the praetor Glaucia and the other partners of his madness [69.5]. Quintus Caecilius Metellus was brought back from exile amid the immense favour of the whole state [69.6].

The proconsul Manius Aquilius brought to an end the slave war which had arisen in Sicily [69.7].
