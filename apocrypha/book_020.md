*Era: 292–219 BCE — Third Samnite aftermath, Pyrrhic War, First Punic War, interwar years.*

The Falisci rebelled; on the sixth day they were thoroughly subdued and surrendered [20.1]. A colony was founded at Spoletium [20.2]. Then for the first time an army was led against the Ligurians [20.3]. The Sardinians and the Corsicans rebelled and were subdued [20.4]. Tuccia, a Vestal virgin, was condemned for unchastity [20.5]. War was declared upon the Illyrians because one of the envoys sent to them had been killed; they were subdued and surrendered [20.6]. The number of the praetors was increased, so that there were four [20.7]. The Transalpine Gauls who had broken into Italy were cut down [20.8]. In that war, it is recorded, the Roman people had under arms, of their own and of the Latin name, eight hundred thousand men [20.9]. Then for the first time Roman armies were led across the Po; the Insubrian Gauls were routed in several battles and surrendered [20.10]. The consul M. Claudius Marcellus killed Vertomarus, leader of the Insubrian Gauls, and brought back the spolia opima [20.11]. The Istrians were subdued; and the Illyrians, who had rebelled again, were tamed and received in surrender [20.12]. The lustrum was closed by the censors three times; at the first, two hundred and seventy thousand two hundred and twelve heads of citizens were counted [20.13]. The freedmen, who before had been dispersed through all the tribes, were confined to four: the Esquiline, the Palatine, the Suburan, and the Colline [20.14]. The censor C. Flaminius built the Flaminian Way and constructed the Flaminian Circus [20.15]. Colonies were founded at Placentia and Cremona, in land captured from the Gauls [20.16].
