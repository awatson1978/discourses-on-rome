A fine was laid upon the praetor Cn. Tremellius, because he had wrongfully contended with the pontifex maximus M. Aemilius Lepidus; and the claim of religion proved stronger than that of the magistrates [47.1]. A law was carried concerning bribery at elections [47.2]. The lustrum was closed by the censors; the heads of citizens counted were three hundred and twenty-eight thousand three hundred and sixteen [47.3]. Aemilius Lepidus was made princeps senatus for the sixth time [47.4]. Between the quarrelling brothers, the Ptolemies, a pact was struck, that the one should reign in Egypt, the other at Cyrene [47.5]. Ariarathes, king of Cappadocia, driven from his kingdom by the scheme and the forces of Demetrius, king of Syria, was restored by the senate [47.6]. Men were sent by the senate to judge between Masinissa and the Carthaginians concerning land [47.7]. The consul C. Marcius fought against the Dalmatians, at first with little success, afterward with better; and the same people was subdued by the consul Cornelius Nasica [47.8]. The consul Q. Opimius subdued the Transalpine Ligurians, who were laying waste Antipolis and Nicaea, towns of the Massiliotes [47.9]. Operations besides were conducted in Spain, with little success, by several commanders [47.10]. In the five hundred and ninety-eighth year from the founding of the city the consuls began to enter upon office on the Kalends of January, the time of the elections being changed because the Spaniards were in rebellion [47.11]. The envoys sent to arbitrate between the Carthaginians and Masinissa reported that they had discovered at Carthage a great quantity of timber for ships [47.12]. Several praetors were accused of avarice in their provinces and condemned [47.13].
