Gaius Caesar, after defeating the Treveri in Gaul, crossed a second time into Germany; and finding no enemy there, he returned into Gaul [107.1]. He defeated the Eburones and the other states that had conspired, and pursued Ambiorix in his flight [107.2].

The body of Publius Clodius, who was killed by Titus Annius Milo, a candidate for the consulship, on the Appian Way at Bovillae, was cremated by the plebs in the senate house [107.3]. Amid the violent struggles among the consular candidates Hypsaeus, Scipio, and Milo, Gnaeus Pompeius was made consul for the third time by the senate, that he might suppress them — in his absence, and as sole consul, which had befallen no other man [107.4]. A court of inquiry having been decreed concerning the death of Publius Clodius, Milo was condemned at trial and driven into exile [107.5].

A law was passed that account be taken of the absent Caesar's candidacy for the consulship, against the will and over the opposition of Marcus Cato [107.6].

In Gaul meanwhile the Gauls revolted almost universally, under the leadership of the Arvernian Vercingetorix; and Caesar carried on operations against them, with laborious sieges of cities, among them Avaricum of the Bituriges and Gergovia of the Arverni [107.7].
