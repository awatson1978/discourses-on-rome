At Rome the praetor Marcus Caelius Rufus, having inflamed the plebs with the hope that their debts would be cancelled, stirred sedition in the city; for which cause he was deposed from his magistracy and driven from Rome, and joining himself to the exile Milo, who had gathered an army of fugitives, he attempted war, and in the attempt both men were killed [111.1].

Abroad, Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, was driven from her kingdom by her brother Ptolemy [111.2]. In Spain, because of the greed and the cruelty of the praetor Quintus Cassius, the people of Corduba, together with the two Varronian legions, fell away from Caesar's side [111.3].
