The consul L. Aurelius subdued the Sardinians, who had rebelled [60.1]. M. Gracchus, a more eloquent man than his brother Tiberius, carried several pernicious laws: a grain law, that grain be furnished to the plebs at six and one-third asses; a second agrarian law, like his brother's; and a third, by which to corrupt the equestrian order, that six hundred equites be added to the three hundred senators in the senate [60.6]. His tribunate being continued into a second year, C. Gracchus by agrarian laws brought about the founding of several colonies in Italy, and of one upon the soil of destroyed Carthage, which he led out himself as triumvir [60.7]. The book also contained the campaign of the consul Q. Metellus against the Baleares, whom the Greeks call Gymnesii, because they pass the summer naked [60.8]. Disturbances in Syria are likewise set forth, in which Cleopatra killed her husband Demetrius, and her son Seleucus also, angered that, upon the killing of his father by her, he had assumed the diadem without her leave [60.9].
