Gaius Caesar laid siege to Massilia, which had closed its gates against him, and left his legates Gaius Trebonius and Decimus Brutus at the siege of the city [110.1]. Setting out for Spain, he received in surrender at Ilerda Lucius Afranius and Marcus Petreius, legates of Gnaeus Pompeius, with seven legions, and let them all go unharmed [110.2]. Varro too, a legate of Pompeius, was brought with his army into Caesar's power [110.3]. To the people of Gades Caesar gave citizenship [110.4]. The Massilienses, defeated in two naval battles, after a long siege surrendered themselves to Caesar's power [110.5].

Gaius Antonius, a legate of Caesar, after operating unsuccessfully against the Pompeians in Illyricum, was captured [110.6]. In that war the Opitergini from across the Po, auxiliaries of Caesar, their raft hemmed in by the enemy's ships, fell by one another's hands rather than come into the power of the enemy [110.7].

Gaius Curio, Caesar's legate in Africa, after fighting successfully against Varus, the commander of the Pompeian party, was cut down with his army by Juba, king of Mauretania [110.8].

Gaius Caesar crossed over to Greece [110.9].
