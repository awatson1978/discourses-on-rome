Gaius Caesar as consul carried a law establishing a court of inquiry against those by whose agency his father had been killed; and prosecuted under that law, Marcus Brutus, Gaius Cassius, and Decimus Brutus were condemned in their absence [120.1].

Asinius Pollio also, and Munatius Plancus, joined the forces of Marcus Antonius with their armies and enlarged them [120.2]. Decimus Brutus, whom the senate had ordered to pursue Antonius, was abandoned by his legions; and by order of Antonius, into whose power he had come, he was killed by a Sequanian [120.3].

Gaius Caesar made peace with Antonius and Lepidus, on these terms: that the three of them should be triumvirs for the settling of the republic for five years, and that each should proscribe his own enemies [120.4]. In that proscription stood very many Roman knights, and the names of one hundred and thirty senators, among them Lucius Paulus, brother of Marcus Lepidus, Lucius Caesar, uncle of Antonius, and Marcus Cicero [120.5]. Cicero was killed by the legionary soldier Popillius, in the sixty-third year of his age; and his head, together with his right hand, was set upon the rostra [120.6].

This book also contains the deeds done by Marcus Brutus in Greece [120.7].
