The consulship that year was not received but seized: Gaius Marius, son of Gaius Marius, was made consul by force before his twentieth year [86.1]. In Africa, Gaius Fabius, whom cruelty and avarice had made hateful, was burned alive in his own headquarters [86.2]. Lucius Philippus, Sulla's legate, seized Sardinia, the praetor Quintus Antonius being driven out and killed [86.3].

Sulla meanwhile, lest the Italian peoples should fear him as one about to strip from them the citizenship and the right of the vote so lately given, struck a treaty with them [86.4]; and so confident was he now of certain victory that the litigants who approached him he ordered to carry their recognizances to Rome, though the city was still held by the opposing party [86.5].

At Rome the praetor Lucius Damasippus, by the will of the consul Gaius Marius, convened the senate and slaughtered all the nobility that was in the city [86.6]. Among them Quintus Mucius Scaevola, the pontifex maximus, as he fled, was cut down in the vestibule of the temple of Vesta [86.7]. In Asia, Lucius Murena renewed the war against Mithridates [86.8].
