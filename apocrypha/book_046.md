*Era: 167–91 BCE — post-Pydna Mediterranean hegemony, Carthage and Corinth destroyed, the Gracchi, Jugurtha, Cimbri, the road to the Social War.*

When king Eumenes, who in the Macedonian war had stood neutral, came to Rome, a law was passed touching all kings alike, that no king should be permitted to come to Rome [46.1]. The consul Claudius Marcellus subdued the Alpine Gauls, the consul C. Sulpicius Gallus the Ligurians [46.2]. Envoys of king Prusias complained of Eumenes, that he was ravaging their borders, and said that he had conspired with Antiochus against the Roman people [46.3]. An alliance was made with the Rhodians at their entreaty [46.4]. The lustrum was closed by the censors; the heads of citizens counted were three hundred and thirty-seven thousand and twenty-two [46.5]. M. Aemilius Lepidus was made princeps senatus [46.6]. Ptolemy, king of Egypt, driven from his kingdom by his younger brother, was restored by envoys sent to him [46.7]. On the death of Ariarathes, king of Cappadocia, his son Ariarathes received the kingdom, and through envoys renewed friendship with the Roman people [46.8]. Campaigns besides were waged, with varying outcome, against the Ligurians, the Corsicans, and the Lusitanians [46.9]. In Syria, disturbed after the death of Antiochus, Demetrius the son of Seleucus, who was held at Rome as a hostage, slipped away secretly; he killed the boy Antiochus together with his guardian Lysias, and was received into the kingdom [46.10]. L. Aemilius Paulus, who had conquered Perseus, died; and so great was his integrity that, though he had brought back immense wealth from Spain and from Macedonia, the auction of his goods scarcely yielded his wife's dowry [46.11]. The Pomptine marshes were drained by the consul Cornelius Cethegus, to whom that province had fallen, and were made into farmland [46.12].
