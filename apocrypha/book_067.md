Marcus Aurelius Scaurus, a legate of consular rank, was captured by the Cimbri after his army was routed; and when he warned them not to cross the Alps, he was killed by the fierce young Boiorix [67.1]. The consul Gnaeus Manlius and the proconsul Quintus Servilius Caepio were defeated by the Cimbri at Arausio and stripped of both their camps; eighty thousand soldiers and forty thousand camp-servants and sutlers were slain, as Antias records [67.2]. Caepio, by whose rashness the disaster had been incurred, was condemned; his property was confiscated, the first so treated since king Tarquinius, and his command was abrogated [67.3].

In the triumph of Gaius Marius, Jugurtha was led before the chariot with his two sons, and was put to death in prison [67.4]. Marius entered the senate in triumphal dress, which no man had done before; and because of fear of the Cimbric war his consulship was continued through several years, the second and third conferred in his absence, the fourth gained while he feigned not to seek it [67.5]. Gnaeus Domitius was made pontifex maximus by vote of the people [67.6].

The Cimbri, after ravaging all that lay between the Rhone and the Pyrenees, crossed into Spain; put to flight there by the Celtiberi, they returned into Gaul and joined the Teutoni among the Veliocasses [67.7].
