Gaius Octavius came to Rome from Epirus, whither Caesar had sent him ahead for the Macedonian war that was intended; he was received with favorable omens, and took the name of Caesar [117.1]. In the confusion and tumult of the time Marcus Lepidus seized the chief pontificate [117.2].

The consul Marcus Antonius bore himself violently in his domination, carried by force a law on the exchange of provinces, and used Caesar with great injuries; and Caesar, that he might gain strength for himself and for the republic against him, roused the veterans who had been settled in the colonies [117.3]. The fourth legion and the Martian carried their standards over from Antonius to Caesar; and afterwards many more fell away, because of the savagery of Marcus Antonius, who butchered suspected men in his camps [117.4]. Decimus Brutus occupied Mutina with his army, to withstand Antonius as he made for Cisalpine Gaul [117.5].

This book also contains the movements of the men of both parties to take up their provinces, and the preparations for war [117.6].
