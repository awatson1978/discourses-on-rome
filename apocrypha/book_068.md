The praetor Marcus Antonius pursued the sea-raiders, that is the pirates, into Cilicia [68.1].

The consul Gaius Marius defended his camp against a fierce assault of the Teutoni and the Ambrones, and then destroyed those enemies in two battles near Aquae Sextiae, in which, by report, two hundred thousand of the enemy were slain and ninety thousand captured [68.2]. Marius was elected consul a fifth time in his absence; the triumph offered him he deferred until he should defeat the Cimbri also [68.3]. The Cimbri, having driven back the proconsul Quintus Catulus and crossed into Italy, were defeated in battle by the joined armies of Catulus and Marius; in that battle, by report, one hundred and forty thousand of the enemy were slain and sixty thousand captured [68.4]. Marius, welcomed by the consent of the whole state, was content with a single triumph in place of the two offered him; and the leading men of the state, who had envied him, acknowledged that the republic had been saved by him [68.5].

Publicius Malleolus, who had killed his mother, was the first to be sewn into a sack and cast into the sea [68.6]. It is recorded that the ancilia moved with a clattering noise before the Cimbric war was brought to its end [68.7]. The book also contains the wars waged between the kings of Syria [68.8].
