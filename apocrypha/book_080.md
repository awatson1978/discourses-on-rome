Citizenship was granted by the senate to the Italian peoples [80.1]. The Samnites, who alone were still in arms, joined themselves to Cinna and Marius; and the legate Plautius was cut down with his army by them [80.2]. Cinna and Marius, with Carbo and Sertorius, attacked the Janiculum, and were put to flight by the consul Octavius and retreated [80.3]. Marius stormed the colonies of Antium, Aricia, and Lanuvium [80.4].

When the optimates had no hope left of resisting, because of the sloth, the treachery, and the corruption of leaders and soldiers alike, Cinna and Marius were received into the city; and they ravaged it as if it were captured, with slaughter and plunder, killing the consul Gnaeus Octavius and butchering all the nobles of the opposing party, among them the orator Marcus Antonius, and Gaius and Lucius Caesar, whose heads were set upon the rostra [80.5]. The son of Crassus was killed by Fimbria's cavalry; Crassus the father, lest he suffer anything unworthy of his valour, ran himself through with his sword [80.6].

Without any elections, Cinna and Marius declared themselves consuls for the following year [80.7]. On the very day on which he entered office, Marius ordered the senator Sextus Licinius to be thrown from the rock; and after committing very many crimes, he died on the Ides of January [80.8].
