The tribune Marcus Livius Drusus, that he might defend the cause of the senate with greater strength, stirred up the allies and the Italian peoples with the hope of Roman citizenship [71.1]. With their help he carried agrarian and grain laws by violence, and carried also a judiciary law, by which the courts were shared equally between the senate and the equestrian order [71.2].

But when the promised citizenship could not be delivered to the allies, the Italians in anger began to agitate defection; and their gatherings, their conspiracies, and the speeches of their chief men in their councils are here reported [71.3].
