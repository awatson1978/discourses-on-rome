*Era: 292–219 BCE — Third Samnite aftermath, Pyrrhic War, First Punic War, interwar years.*

The consul Valerius Laevinus fought with ill success against Pyrrhus, the soldiers being terrified above all by the unaccustomed appearance of the elephants [13.1]. When the battle was done and Pyrrhus inspected the bodies of the Romans who had fallen, he found every wound facing the enemy; and he advanced, plundering as he went, toward the city of Rome [13.2]. C. Fabricius was sent by the senate to treat for the ransom of the captives; the king tempted him in vain to desert his country, and the captives were returned without price [13.3]. Cineas, sent by Pyrrhus as envoy to the senate to ask that the king be received into the city to settle a peace, was refused, after Appius Claudius had come into the senate-house and carried his opinion [13.4]. The censor Cn. Domitius, the first man of the plebs to close the lustrum, held the census; the heads of citizens counted were two hundred and eighty-seven thousand two hundred and twenty-two [13.5]. A second battle was fought against Pyrrhus, with outcome undecided [13.6]. The treaty with the Carthaginians was renewed for the fourth time [13.7]. When a deserter from Pyrrhus promised the consul C. Fabricius that he would give the king poison, the man was sent back to the king with a report of his offer [13.8]. Campaigns besides were waged with success against the Lucanians, the Bruttii, the Samnites, and the Etruscans [13.9].
